## 1. Evelyn Grace Academy

- **Architect and place:** Zaha Hadid Architects, Brixton, London Borough of Lambeth, UK.
- **Year:** opened 13 September 2010. The ZHA page gives the dates as 2006 to 2010.
- **Sources opened:**
  - https://www.archiweb.cz/en/n/foreign/gymnazium-v-brixtonu-od-zahy-hadid: "the one hundred meter long red running track that runs from north to south, crossing through the school building"; opened 13 September 2010; designed for 1,200 pupils; ZHA with Arup and QS Davis Langdon.
  - https://www.zha.com/architecture/evelyn-grace-academy/?disclaimer=true (the zaha-hadid.com address redirects here): Brixton; dates 2006 to 2010; client ARK; "schools within schools", four schools sharing communal spaces. The only track text is a photo caption, "Running track from Shakespeare Road entrance".
  - https://www.bdonline.co.uk/buildings/evelyn-grace-academy-brixton-by-zaha-hadid-architects/5007604.article: "A 100m race track cuts across the plan linking the two site entrances"; article dated 22 October 2010.
  - https://www.zhfoundation.com/collections/evelyn-grace-academy/: a second "stitch" across the site, two parallel cuts running almost perpendicular to the building, became the 100 m red track; the track is also a route into and entrance to the school.
- **Number used:** 100 m track (Archiweb).
- **Not verified:** that the track passes "under" the building. Domus (https://domusweb.it/en/architecture/2010/10/28/evelyn-grace-academy-london-by-zaha-hadid.html) returned 403.

## 2. Frederiksbjerg School

- **Architect and place:** Henning Larsen Architects with GPP Arkitekter; landscape by Møller & Grønborg. Ingerslevs Boulevard 2, Aarhus, Denmark.
- **Year:** built 2016, opened August 2016.
- **Sources opened:**
  - https://dac.dk/en/magazine/places/frederiksbjerg-school-357: more than 900 pupils; "several climbing walls, swings, colored paths on the floors, and wall bars"; "no less than 40 zones that facilitate activity throughout the building" each day; access to each floor by the main stairs; a minimum of 45 minutes of physical activity in the timetable.
- **Number used:** more than 900 pupils (dac.dk).
- **Not verified:** "active routes between lessons". dac.dk does not describe routes between lessons. The architect's own site (henninglarsen.com) failed to resolve twice (DNS error). A Gratnells brochure that appeared in search was not opened and is not used.

## 3. YueCheng Courtyard Kindergarten

- **Architect and place:** MAD Architects, Beijing, China (district not stated).
- **Year:** completed at the end of 2019. Divisare lists the project year as 2020.
- **Source opened:**
  - https://divisare.com/projects/433427-mad-architects-hufton-crow-tian-fangfang-arch-exist-iwan-baan-creatar-images-yuecheng-courtyard-kindergarten: 390 children aged 1.5 to 6; an undulating "floating" roof that works as a playground; "slides and stairs connect the courtyards to the rooftop landscape above".
- **Number used:** 390 children (Divisare).
- **Not verified:** the roof colour (Dezeen gave red and orange in search results, but the Dezeen page returned 403). Ramps are not mentioned.

## 4. Tiantai No. 2 Primary School

- **Architect and place:** LYCS Architecture (designboom names Zhejiang University Urban Research & Design Institute as architect of record). Tiantai, Zhejiang Province, China.
- **Year:** construction dates 2013 to 2014. Article dated 21 October 2014 (stadiumdb).
- **Sources opened:**
  - https://designboom.com/?p=215772: the school is "based on the form of a 200-meter running track"; the four-storey building's oval shape follows the rooftop track; construction 2013 to 2014.
  - https://stadiumdb.com/news/2014/10/china_school_pretending_to_be_a_stadium_and_doing_it_well: "the school covers three floors and atop it are the running track and both playing fields"; "the running track went up to the roof and became the future school's blueprint."
- **Number used:** 200 m rooftop track (designboom).
- **Not verified:** the floor count (four or three). The land-scarcity claim and the 3,000 m² figure came only from search snippets.

## 5. Fuji Kindergarten

- **Architect and place:** Tezuka Architects (Takaharu and Yui Tezuka), Tachikawa, Tokyo, Japan.
- **Year:** completed 2007. Construction ran from March 2006 to January 2007.
- **Sources opened:**
  - https://www.e-architect.com/japan/roof-house: a "distorted oval" roof, sloped for drainage, with no play equipment; trees project through openings surrounded by nets; children "race around this roof".
  - https://www.azuremagazine.com/?p=90308: up to 600 children, including children with autism; a single storey, "circular and oval-shaped"; "The rooftop itself becomes the play equipment."; "Even children who don't usually run will race around this roof."
- **Number used:** up to 600 children (Azure).
- **Not verified:** the research/04 Dezeen link (https://www.dezeen.com/2017/10/02/fuji-kindergarten-tokyo-tezuka-architects-oval-roof-deck-playground/) returned 403 and was not relied on. "No start and no end" is not in any page opened.

## 6. Farming Kindergarten

- **Architect and place:** Vo Trong Nghia Architects (Vo Trong Nghia, Takashi Niwa, Masaaki Iwamoto). Biên Hòa town, Dong Nai province, Vietnam.
- **Year:** 2013 (Arch2O project year). designboom's article is dated 30 April 2014 and does not state a completion year.
- **Sources opened:**
  - https://www.arch2o.com/?p=133390: project year 2013; "a triple-ring shape drawn with a single stroke, encircling three courtyards inside as safe playgrounds"; 500 preschool children; a 200 m² roof garden with five vegetables planted for agricultural education.
  - https://www.designboom.com/architecture/vo-trong-nghia-architects-farming-kindergarten-vietnam-04-30-2014/: a "fully accessible green roof drawn in a triple-ring-shape creates three courtyards"; 500 children.
  - https://mymodernmet.com/green-roof-kindergarten-vietnam: "recently completed"; a "spiraling knot of a roof" that stays green all year; the loop lands flush with the ground to make covered courtyards.
- **Number used:** 200 m² roof garden (Arch2O).
- **Not verified:** the 2013 completion year rests on Arch2O alone. The Dezeen 2013 and 2014 articles returned 403.

## 7. Cathedral Grammar Junior School

- **Architect and place:** Andrew Barrie Lab with Tezuka Architects (Takaharu Tezuka). Christchurch, New Zealand.
- **Year:** opened 2016 (school site). Andrew Barrie Lab gives the project period as 2013 to 2016.
- **Sources opened:**
  - https://www.andrewbarrielab.com/cathedral-grammar-junior-school: seven classrooms; "A stair leads to a large roof deck, and a slide running back down makes the building itself an adventure for the children."; architects Andrew Barrie Lab and Tezuka Architects.
  - https://cathedralgrammar.school.nz/why-choose-us/location-and-facilities/: Junior School opened 2016; a classroom block with a roof deck overlooking a park; "a slide from the rooftop to a green courtyard below".
  - https://www.nzia.co.nz/awards/national/award-detail/7819: credits Andrew Barrie Lab and Tezuka Architects. The award year is unclear (the page title says 2018, the logo says 2024), so no award is claimed.
- **Number used:** seven classrooms (Andrew Barrie Lab).
- **Not verified:** the Architecture Now article returned a 525 error.

## 8. School of Dancing Arches

- **Architect and place:** Samira Rathod Design Associates (principal Samira Rathod), Bhadran, Anand District, Gujarat, India.
- **Year:** project years 2014 to 2019 (Brick Architecture). Articles dated 26 August 2019 (World Architecture) and 19 April 2020 (Designessentia).
- **Sources opened:**
  - https://brickarchitecture.com/projects/school-of-dancing-arches-samira-rathod-design-associates: "tilted vaults and bricked-arches"; modules that each have "a pair of classrooms and a corridor"; "conceptualized from a child's early scribbles that turned into a series of dancing arches".
  - https://designessentiamagazine.com/school-of-dancing-arches-srda/: built; floor area 18,000 sq ft; "terracotta, bricks and only bricks in walls, floors, and roofs"; the bricks come from a kiln close to the site.
  - https://worldarchitecture.org/architecture-news/ecmzn/a-school-built-from-tilted-brick-vaults-evoke-a-child-s-freedom-in-india.html: Mumbai-based Samira Rathod Design Associates; modules with a pair of classrooms and a corridor.
- **Number used:** 18,000 sq ft (Designessentia).
- **Not verified:** "eleven classrooms", "Samira Rathod Design Atelier" and the Dezeen 2020 longlisting came only from search snippets. The Dezeen 2019 article returned 403.

## 9. Pitisuksa Chiang Rai School (Airport Campus)

- **Architect and place:** 1922 Architects (Songtam Srinakarin and Piangor Pattayakorn). Landscape master plan by Chulaphon Nanthapanich of North Forest Studio. Chiang Rai, Thailand.
- **Year:** not stated. The article is dated 23 March 2026, and that is the year used.
- **Source opened:**
  - https://art4d.com/en/2026/03/pitisuksa-chiang-rai-school-airport-campus: six classrooms; three buildings (administration, a bamboo multi-purpose pavilion and the classroom building); "two ponds linked by a meandering canal that runs longitudinally through the site"; the first pond is near the entrance "to give children a choice in how they arrive at school"; paths alternate between open-air and covered walkways.
- **Number used:** six classrooms (art4d).
- **Not verified:** that the canal lies between classrooms; the completion year; the "11 acres" figure (search snippet only).

## 10. Gangjin Children's Centre

- **Architect and place:** JYA-rchitects. Maryang village, Gangjin, South Korea.
- **Year:** 2013. Work started in 2012 and finished in 2013 (Archilovers).
- **Sources opened:**
  - https://www.archdaily.com/345181/gangjin-children-center-jya-rchitects: completion year 2013; total area 223 m². The fetched copy is mostly captions.
  - https://www.archilovers.com/projects/80984/gangjin-sannaedeul-children-center.html: a hurricane in summer 2012 destroyed the earlier centre; children's drawings of "house-shaped" buildings; "a dynamic-shaped roof"; "the overlapped roof has also brought up the image of the sea waves"; "the study room on the second floor was connected to a terrace with a slide"; "the most beautiful seaside view".
- **Number used:** 223 m² total area (ArchDaily).
- **Not verified:** the word "zigzag"; "built in under a year" (search snippet only). The Dezeen 2013 article returned 403 twice.
