# Numbers used in Astra Architect

Checked 12 Oct 2026. One line per number: the number, the six-year-old comparison, the source URL, and the date checked. Counts used as instructions are listed at the end.

## Numbers on cards

- 62 square metres: a reception or infant classroom for 30 pupils. Comparison: a square room, about the size of a school classroom. Source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f23ec238fa8f57acac33720/BB103_Area_Guidelines_for_Mainstream_Schools.pdf (BB103, schedule rows PRI33 and PRA26). Checked 12 Oct 2026.
- 55 square metres: a junior classroom for 30 pupils (BB103 row PRI32). Standard classrooms are 55 to 62 square metres for 30 pupils (BB103 basic teaching text). Comparison: a square room a little smaller than the reception one. Source: the same BB103 link. Checked 12 Oct 2026.
- About 8 metres across: DERIVED, not published. A square of 62 square metres has sides of about 7.9 metres, rounded to 8. It assumes the classroom is square. Comparison: a square room you could walk across in a few steps. Source: BB103 for the area, the square is our model. Checked 12 Oct 2026.
- About 1.15 metres (about 115 cm): a typical six-year-old's height, the median (50th centile) at 72 months. Boys 115.95 cm, girls 115.12 cm. Comparison: lying head to toe, one of these takes up about a metre and a bit of floor. Sources: https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/child-growth/growth-reference-5-19-years/height-for-age-(5-19-years)/hfa-boys-5-19years-per.pdf and https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/child-growth/growth-reference-5-19-years/height-for-age-(5-19-years)/hfa-girls-5-19years-per.pdf (WHO height-for-age, 5 to 19 years, row 6:0 = 72 months, column M which is the median when L is 1). Checked 12 Oct 2026. Caveat: the RCPCH UK-WHO 2 to 18 chart page gave no figures and its charts are image PDFs that could not be read; the NHS height page returned 404. The WHO reference is the one I could read, so check the RCPCH chart if you want the UK chart figure.
- About 7 (the answer): DERIVED. About 8 divided by about 1.15 is about 7. Comparison: seven six-year-olds in a row, head to toe. Derived from the two lines above.
- 2, 7 and 30 (guess choices): 2 and 30 are wrong answers. 30 is the class size used in the BB103 rows ("for 30"). Comparison: two people, or a whole class. Source: the BB103 link. Checked 12 Oct 2026.
- Three ways to draw a building (plan, section, elevation): comparison: three pictures of one toy, from above, cut through the middle and from the side. Source: https://digitaldesign.aalto.fi/digital-design-workflows/vector/2d-cad-drafting/drawing-2d-section-and-projection/ (Aalto University, definitions of plan, section and elevation). Checked 12 Oct 2026.

## Other sources cited (no numbers on the card)

- https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/questions/question14.html (NASA Goddard StarChild): "The Sun, the Moon, the planets, and the stars all rise in the east." Used for the Mission 1 sun guess. Checked 12 Oct 2026.
- https://cabinetmagazine.org/issues/9/brosterman.php: Frank Lloyd Wright describes playing with smooth maple-wood blocks at kindergarten and says he could still feel them in his fingers. Used for the Mission 4 story. Checked 12 Oct 2026. Note: the article does not name Froebel's gifts directly.
- https://froebelweb.org/fblgaben.html: Froebel's first gifts were three solids, sphere, cube and cylinder, for young children to play with. Verifies the Mission 4 story's Froebel claim. Checked 12 Oct 2026.
- https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/buildingbig/educator/act_mini_arch.html: an arch turns the weight above into a push along its curve, balanced by an inward force, and a human arch activity where students push against each other's hands. Used for the Mission 4 arch guess. Caveat: this page does not use the words "stones" or "glue"; the reveal's "stones push against each other" is the standard explanation that the page's thrust description supports.
- https://www.outdoordesign.com.au/news-info/tokyo-roof-run/5669: Fuji Kindergarten in Tokyo, Tezuka Architects, an oval roof with endless laps and "no sense of a middle". Used for the Mission 4 story. Checked 12 Oct 2026. (The Dezeen article returned 403.)
- BB103 (same PDF as above): "changing rooms with showers, near to indoor and outdoor sports provision". Used for the Mission 2 pool guess. Checked 12 Oct 2026.
