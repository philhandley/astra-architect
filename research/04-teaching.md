# Teaching architecture and coding to a six-year-old

Checked 12 Oct 2026. Sources are given so cards can cite them. Where the agent could not open
a page it says so.

## What children's architecture programmes actually do at 5 to 7

- RIBA National Schools Programme: Years 1 and 2 do "Architecture Adventures" workshops, then
  invent buildings. Buildings have stories and places.
  https://www.riba.org/learn/public-and-school-learning/schools/national-schools-programme/
- Open City, Architecture in Schools (Years 4 to 6, but the method scales down): visit the
  site, measure yourself against the building, draw, then model. Scale, and plan to model.
  https://open-city.org.uk/blog/architecture-in-schools-in-more-detail
- AA Little Architect (ages 5 to 9): "Rethinking Your School" workshops, redesigning the places
  children use daily. https://www.archdaily.com/794775/
- Arkki, Helsinki (ages 4 to 19): hut building and model making; play builds 3D thinking.
  https://www.arkki.fi/en/
- Archikidz: a shared dream neighbourhood built together as a model.
  https://benthemcrouwel.com/news/architectural-playgrounds-kids-create-their-dream-neighborhoods-at-archikidz
- Froebel's Gifts: sphere, cube and cylinder to turn and touch, then building blocks, "from
  solid to plane to line to point". Frank Lloyd Wright credited his childhood maple blocks with
  his sense of structure. https://froebelweb.org/fblgaben.html and
  https://cabinetmagazine.org/issues/9/brosterman.php
- Fuji Kindergarten, Tezuka Architects, Tokyo 2007: an oval roof deck children run round, with
  no start and no end. https://www.dezeen.com/2017/10/02/fuji-kindergarten-tokyo-tezuka-architects-oval-roof-deck-playground/

## Coding at 5 to 7: what works

- Appropriate: sequence, a small counted repeat, debugging by running and fixing one tile,
  predicting the path before pressing Go. Avoid variables, text conditionals and typed numbers.
- ScratchJr: picture-only blocks, one colour per category (start, motion, looks, sound,
  control, end). Bee-Bot: a pause command, a beep and blink on every step, one Clear.
  Cubetto: no text or digits, one Go button. Kodable: "forward five times" as one tile.
  Code.org unplugged: pieces must touch and run in order.
- Evidence is thin but consistent: Tufts DevTech work on ScratchJr in K to 2 (sequencing errors
  more common than block recall), small Bee-Bot classroom studies.
  https://github.com/LLK/scratchjr (BSD, archived); https://www.kodable.com/coding-for-kids

## Touch and usability at 5 to 7

- Big targets, single taps and single-finger drags; no pinch, no double tap, no multi-finger.
  https://developers.google.com/building-for-kids/designing-engaging-apps
- Tap-then-tap placement beats drag for the youngest (fingers lift mid-drag).
  https://oldmagazine.uxpa.org/?p=11776
- Keep controls off the bottom edge where resting hands sit; tapping is the most intuitive
  input. Sesame Workshop 2012: https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/?p=19607
- Non-readers need spoken prompts and icons; avoid scrolling. NN/g:
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/childrens-websites-usability-issues/
- Missed targets are common at 4 to 7 (study of 28 children):
  https://init.cise.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/378/2017/03/chen-et-al-ICMI2020.pdf
- Undo for every placement (Townscaper, Islanders).

## Real schools for the Ideas book (beyond the six on Phil's sheet)

1. Cathedral Grammar Junior School, Christchurch, 2016, Andrew Barrie Lab with Tezuka: seven
   classrooms for ages 5 to 7, a stair to a roof deck and a slide back down to the courtyard.
   https://www.andrewbarrielab.com/cathedral-grammar-junior-school
2. School of Dancing Arches, Bhadran, India, 2019, Samira Rathod Design Associates: arches
   that grew from a child's scribbles; each block holds two classrooms and a corridor.
   https://brickarchitecture.com/projects/school-of-dancing-arches-samira-rathod-design-associates
3. Pitisuksa Chiang Rai School, Airport Campus, Thailand, 1922 Architects (published 2026):
   two ponds joined by a winding canal between six classrooms.
   https://art4d.com/en/2026/03/pitisuksa-chiang-rai-school-airport-campus
4. Gangjin Children's Centre, Maryang, South Korea, 2013, JYA-rchitects: zigzag roofs, a roof
   terrace and a slide with a sea view, designed from local children's drawings.
   https://www.archdaily.com/345181/gangjin-children-center-jya-rchitects
